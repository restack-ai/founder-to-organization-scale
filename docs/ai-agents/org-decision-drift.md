# AI Agents and Organizational Decision Drift

AI agents change the shape of organizational scale.

They do not simply automate work. They redistribute how decisions are prepared, recommended, routed, and sometimes executed.

## Pre-agent organization

```text
Human gathers context → human drafts proposal → human asks meeting → human waits → human executes
```

## Agent-augmented organization

```text
Human sets intent → agent gathers context → agent drafts options → human reviews threshold → agent routes / executes bounded work → logs and evals update
```

## Drift 1: Decentralization of decision preparation

Agents let more people prepare higher-quality decisions because they can:

- summarize documents;
- synthesize customer feedback;
- inspect metrics;
- draft decision memos;
- compare options;
- prepare implementation plans;
- route work to the right owner.

This can make ownership-driven culture easier because the cost of context preparation drops.

## Drift 2: Recentralization of governance

At the same time, agentic work requires centralized policies:

- what tools agents may call;
- what data classes they may access;
- what actions require approval;
- what outputs need evals;
- what logs must be retained;
- who is accountable when an agent acts.

So AI agents create a paradox:

> Decision preparation decentralizes, but governance infrastructure centralizes.

## Drift 3: Founder bottleneck becomes policy bottleneck

In founder-driven companies, people wait for the founder.

In poorly designed AI-agent companies, people wait for unclear security, compliance, and approval policies.

The new bottleneck may be:

- no permission model;
- unclear human approval thresholds;
- weak evals;
- no audit trail;
- no data classification;
- no owner for agent failures.

## Strategic question

The real question is not:

> Can agents replace managers?

The better question is:

> Can agents make decision ownership safer, faster, and more observable?
